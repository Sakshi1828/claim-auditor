  TEST FIXTURE — NOT REAL RESEARCH

  All study names, cohort counts, effect estimates, p-values, sample
  IDs, and results below are invented for software testing. They do not
  describe actual patients or findings. Do not cite this document as
  scientific evidence.

Purpose and test instructions

Feed this report into your application as an untrusted research
document. Ask the system to audit every numbered claim, trace each claim
to the cited table or stated source, identify whether the evidence is
sufficient, and produce a plain-language summary with a recommended
research workflow next step. The system should not turn these findings
into diagnosis, treatment advice, or patient risk scores.

Expected challenge: This report intentionally mixes supported, partially
supported, unsupported, conflicting, and unverifiable claims. A strong
system should not mark every claim as true or false. It should explain
what is supported, what is missing, and what would be needed to verify
it.

1. Research question and scope

Working title: Exploratory assessment of molecular subtype labels and
immune-expression measurements in a fictional TCGA-BRCA-like research
cohort.

Research question: In this synthetic breast-tumor research cohort, do
the available molecular subtype labels and immune-expression
measurements support a descriptive comparison of immune-related signals
across subtype groups?

Intended use: Exploratory, aggregate-level research review only. This is
not a clinical validation study and is not intended for diagnosis,
treatment selection, prognosis, or individual-level risk prediction.

Population represented: Fictional de-identified tumor-sample records
assembled for this test fixture. No real patient records are included.

2. Methods and data description

The fictional analysis begins with 120 tumor-sample records. A subtype
label is available for 108 records. An immune-expression summary score
is available for 96 records. Both subtype and immune score are available
for 91 records. A second expression-derived subtype call is available
for 84 records. The report does not provide a data dictionary, release
identifier, source manifest, checksum, or documented join key.

The analyst states that records were matched across files using a sample
barcode, but does not specify the barcode level (participant, sample,
aliquot, or portion), deduplication rules, or whether multiple aliquots
from one participant were retained. This omission is deliberate and
should be flagged as a reproducibility and unit-of-analysis limitation.

4. Claims to audit

The following numbered statements are the claims made by the fictional
report. Treat each as a separate auditable unit. The quoted evidence
references are internal references to sections/tables in this test
document, not external publications.

C1 — Supported at the descriptive level

Claim: “Subtype A is available for 108 of the 120 tumor-sample records
(90.0%).”

Evidence cited: Table 1, row “Subtype label A”.

Audit focus: Check arithmetic and confirm the claim is limited to
availability in this fixture, not biological validity.

C2 — Partially supported

Claim: “The immune-expression score is available for most records in the
cohort.”

Evidence cited: Table 1, row “Immune-expression score”.

Audit focus: 80% is a measurable majority, but “most” is imprecise. Ask
the system to report the exact denominator and distinguish availability
from data quality.

C3 — Unsupported / overclaim

Claim: “The subtype groups have significantly different immune
activity.”

Evidence cited: No group-level values, statistical test, effect size,
confidence interval, or p-value is supplied.

Audit focus: The report provides only overall availability counts. It
does not provide a comparison. The system should not infer a difference.

C4 — Conflicting evidence

Claim: “The cohort has a stable molecular subtype assignment.”

Evidence cited: Methods paragraph says two subtype calls exist; 84
records have a second call. No agreement table or adjudication rule is
supplied.

Audit focus: Two calls may disagree, but disagreement is not
demonstrated. Mark as unverified / potential conflict, not confirmed
contradiction. Request cross-tabulation and agreement metrics.

C5 — Unverifiable due to missing provenance

Claim: “The subtype labels were generated using the validated version 3
pipeline.”

Evidence cited: No pipeline name, version, validation record, or source
manifest appears in the report.

Audit focus: The claim has no supporting evidence in the supplied
document. Do not assume the pipeline version.

C6 — Unsupported causal claim

Claim: “Lower immune-expression scores cause the observed subtype
differences.”

Evidence cited: No causal design, temporal evidence, adjustment
strategy, or subtype-group results are supplied.

Audit focus: Neither the claimed differences nor causality can be
established from the described information.

C7 — Partially supported, denominator-sensitive

Claim: “Joining the subtype and immune-score files retains 75.8% of the
starting cohort.”

Evidence cited: Table 1: 91 records with both fields out of 120 starting
records.

Audit focus: Arithmetic is correct (91/120 = 75.8%) if the records are
on the same unit and the join is valid. Unit-of-analysis and join
behavior are not documented.

C8 — Unsupported generalization

Claim: “These results represent all breast cancer patients.”

Evidence cited: The report describes a fictional sample-level research
cohort and provides no sampling frame or representativeness analysis.

Audit focus: The conclusion exceeds the stated scope. Do not generalize
to all patients or clinical populations.

5. Internal consistency checks

-   Check A: 108/120 = 90.0%; 96/120 = 80.0%; 91/120 = 75.8%; 84/120 =
    70.0%. These calculations are arithmetically consistent when 120 is
    the denominator.
-   Check B: The 91 records with both subtype A and immune score cannot
    be assumed to be 91 unique participants. The report calls them
    tumor-sample records and does not establish participant-level
    uniqueness.
-   Check C: The existence of two subtype calls is not, by itself, proof
    that they conflict. A valid conflict assessment requires paired
    calls on the same defined unit and a comparison table.
-   Check D: Availability counts do not establish score validity,
    measurement quality, biological meaning, or group differences.

6. Missing information and limitations

-   No frozen data release name, manifest, checksums, or file-level
    provenance.
-   No data dictionary or definition of the subtype and
    immune-expression variables.
-   No documented barcode level, join key, duplicate handling, or unit
    of analysis.
-   No group-level summary statistics for immune-expression scores.
-   No statistical test, effect size, confidence interval, or
    multiplicity approach.
-   No method/version details for either subtype call and no agreement
    analysis.
-   No missingness analysis by subtype or other relevant group.
-   No cohort inclusion/exclusion criteria or representativeness
    assessment.
-   No reproducible code, query, notebook, or execution environment.
-   No independent human reviewer or review decision recorded.

7. Possible research-workflow next steps

These are proposed verification steps for the fictional research
workflow, not medical recommendations:

1.  Verify provenance: Obtain the frozen release identifier, source
    manifest, checksums, data dictionary, and pipeline documentation.
2.  Verify the unit and joins: Document whether each row represents a
    participant, sample, aliquot, or portion; rerun the join with
    explicit deduplication rules.
3.  Resolve subtype-call uncertainty: Compare both subtype calls on the
    same matched records; report a cross-tabulation and an appropriate
    agreement statistic.
4.  Test the group-comparison claim: If the research question remains
    appropriate, provide group sizes, descriptive distributions, a
    prespecified statistical method, effect estimates, uncertainty
    intervals, and limitations.
5.  Require human review: A research reviewer should inspect the source
    mapping, join logic, analysis code, and limitations before the
    findings are used in further research.

8. Questions the Evidence Copilot should answer

1.  Which claims are directly supported by the supplied report, and what
    exact evidence supports each one?
2.  Which claims are only partially supported, and what qualification is
    needed?
3.  Which claims are unsupported or overstate the evidence?
4.  Is there a demonstrated conflict between the two subtype calls, or
    only a missing agreement analysis?
5.  Can the report support the claim that immune-expression differs by
    subtype? Why or why not?
6.  What can be reproduced from the supplied numbers alone?
7.  What additional fields, source files, or analysis outputs are needed
    before the key claims can be checked?
8.  What is the safest next research-workflow step, and who should
    review it?
9.  Explain the overall result to a non-technical audience in no more
    than 120 words.
